Paraphrasing and summarizing without losing the source

This page walks you through using a short Occupational Outlook Handbook excerpt, understanding it first, then writing a paraphrase and a summary that are clearly in your own sentence style.

Step 1

Start with a short source excerpt

Use about 5 sentences from the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook page for the career you are researching. Try not to choose an excerpt that is too short because you need enough source material to practice both paraphrasing and summarizing.

Good places to choose from: the Summary, What They Do, Pay, Job Outlook, Work Environment, or How to Become One sections. Choose one section and copy about 5 connected sentences.

Step 2

Understand before you rewrite

A paraphrase should not start with replacing words one at a time. First, make sure you can explain what the source is saying.

0 words. Aim for about 15 to 30 words.
A useful rule

Names, job titles, technical terms, and numbers may stay close to the source. The sentence shape around them should change.

Step 4

Write a paraphrase

A paraphrase keeps the full meaning but changes the sentence structure and wording. Your paraphrase should cover the same main information as the source excerpt, not just one sentence from it. It still needs a citation because the idea came from the source.

Set the source aside

Try hiding the source for 90 seconds, then write from memory. This makes it harder to copy the original sentence shape.

Step 5

Write a summary

For your summary, start with the Main idea. Then add only the Detail that feels necessary.

A summary is not a paraphrase of every sentence. It keeps the main idea and leaves out smaller details, examples, and repeated information.

Label each source sentence

Use the drop-down menu beside each sentence to decide what role that sentence plays in the source.

What the labels mean

Main = the biggest idea the summary should probably keep. Detail = supporting information that may or may not be needed. Example = a specific case or illustration that can usually be left out. Background = context that helps the source make sense but may not belong in a short summary.
